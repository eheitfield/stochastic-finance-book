Appendix A — Properties of Normally Distributed Random Variables

Throughout this appendix, let

\[ X \sim N(\mu\,\sigma^2) \]

denote a normally distributed random variable with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\). The standard normal random variable is denoted by

\[ Z \sim N(0,1). \]

The standard normal probability density function and cumulative distribution function are

\[ \phi(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-z^2/2}, \]

and

\[ \Phi(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{z}\phi(u)\,du\, \]

respectively.

A.1 Basic Properties

Theorem A.1 (Basic Properties of the Normal Distribution) If \(X\sim N(\mu\,\sigma^2)\), then

\[ \E[X]=\mu, \]

\[ \Var[X]=\sigma^2. \]

Furthermore,

\[ \Phi(-z)=1-\Phi(z). \]

A.2 Linear Transformations and Linear Combinations

The following theorem is the fundamental structural property of the normal distribution. Many familiar results are immediate consequences.

Theorem A.2 (Linear Combinations of Jointly Normal Random Variables) Let

\[ X=(X_1\,\ldots\,X_n)' \]

be jointly normally distributed with mean vector \(\mu\) and covariance matrix \(\Sigma\). Then every linear combination

\[ Y=a'X+b \]

is normally distributed with

\[ Y \sim N(a^T\mu+b,\, a^T\Sigma a). \]

The following corollaries are used frequently throughout this book.

Corollary A.1 (Affine Transformation) If

\[ X\sim N(\mu,\,\sigma^2)\, \]

then

\[ aX+b \sim N(a\mu+b,\,a^2\sigma^2). \]

Corollary A.2 (Standardization) If

\[ X\sim N(\mu,\,\sigma^2), \]

then

\[ \frac{X-\mu}{\sigma} \sim N(0\,1). \]

Corollary A.3 (Sum of Independent Normal Random Variables) If

\[ X_i\sim N(\mu_i,\,\sigma_i^2)\, \qquad i=1,\,\ldots,\,n, \]

are independent, then

\[ \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i \sim N\left( \sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_i,\, \sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_i^2 \right). \]

Corollary A.4 (Sum of Correlated Normal Random Variables) If \(X\) and \(Y\) are jointly normal, then

\[ X+Y \]

is normally distributed with

\[ \E[X+Y] = \E[X]+\E[Y], \]

and

\[ \operatorname{Var}(X+Y) = \operatorname{Var}(X) + \operatorname{Var}(Y) + 2\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y). \]

A.3 Noncentral Moments

Theorem A.3 (Noncentral Moments of a Standard Normal Random Variable) If \[ Z\sim N(0,1) \] and \(n\) is a positive integer, then

\[ \E[Z^n] = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } n \text{ is odd} \\ \frac{n!}{2^{n/2} (n/2)!} & \text{if } n \text{ is even} \end{cases}. \] More generally, if

\[ X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2), \] then \[ \mathbb{E}[X^n] = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} \mu^{n-k} \sigma^k \mathbb{E}[Z^k]. \]

A.4 Exponential Moments

The following identity is one of the most useful properties of the normal distribution in mathematical finance. It is used repeatedly in the derivation of geometric Brownian motion, risk-neutral pricing, and the Black–Scholes option pricing formula.

Theorem A.4 (Exponential Moments) If

\[ X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2), \]

then for every real number \(t\),

\[ \E[e^{tX}] = \exp\left( t\mu+\frac12t^2\sigma^2 \right). \]

Two important special cases are

\[ M_X(t) = \E[e^{tX}] = \exp\left( t\mu+\frac12t^2\sigma^2 \right), \]

which is the moment generating function of \(X\), and

\[ \E[e^X] = e^{\mu+\frac12\sigma^2}. \]

A.5 Truncated Normal Expectations

Many option pricing problems require expectations conditioned on a normal random variable lying above or below a threshold.

Define

\[ \alpha=\frac{c-\mu}{\sigma}. \]

Theorem A.5 (Tail Probability) If

\[ X\sim N(\mu,\,\sigma^2), \]

then

\[ P(X>c) = 1-\Phi(\alpha). \]

Theorem A.6 (Conditional Mean Above a Threshold) If

\[ X\sim N(\mu,\,\sigma^2), \]

then

\[ \E[X\mid X>c] = \mu + \sigma \frac{\phi(\alpha)} {1-\Phi(\alpha)}. \]

Theorem A.7 (Conditional Mean Below a Threshold) If

\[ X\sim N(\mu,\,\sigma^2), \]

then

\[ \E[X\mid X<c] = \mu - \sigma \frac{\phi(\alpha)} {\Phi(\alpha)}. \]

Theorem A.8 (Exponential Tail Expectation) If

\[ X\sim N(\mu,\,\sigma^2), \]

then

\[ \E\left[ e^{tX} \mathbf{1}_{{X>c}} \right] = \exp\left( t\mu+\frac12t^2\sigma^2 \right) \Phi\left( \frac{\mu+t\sigma^2-c}{\sigma} \right). \]

In particular,

\[ \E\left[ e^{X} \mathbf{1}_{{X>c}} \right] = e^{\mu+\frac12\sigma^2} \Phi\left( \frac{\mu+\sigma^2-c}{\sigma} \right). \]

A.6 The Lognormal Distribution

The exponential of a normally distributed random variable has a lognormal distribution.

Theorem A.9 (Relationship Between the Normal and Lognormal Distributions) Suppose

\[ X \sim N(\mu,\,\sigma^2)\, \]

and define

\[ Y=e^X. \]

Then \(Y\) is lognormally distributed, written

\[ Y \sim \operatorname{LogNormal}(\mu,\,\sigma^2). \]

Conversely, if

\[ Y \sim \operatorname{LogNormal}(\mu,\,\sigma^2), \]

then

\[ \ln Y \sim N(\mu,\,\sigma^2). \]

Theorem A.10 (Moments of the Lognormal Distribution) If

\[ Y \sim \operatorname{LogNormal}(\mu,\,\sigma^2)\, \]

then

\[ E[Y] = e^{\mu+\frac12\sigma^2}, \]

and

\[ \operatorname{Var}(Y) = \left(e^{\sigma^2}-1\right) e^{2\mu+\sigma^2}. \]

More generally,

\[ E[Y^t] = \exp\!\left( t\mu+\frac12t^2\sigma^2 \right) \]

for every real number \(t\).

A.7 Truncated Lognormal Expectations

Suppose

\[ Y \sim \operatorname{LogNormal}(\mu,\,\sigma^2)\, \]

and let \(K>0\).

Theorem A.11 (Lognormal Tail Probability) The probability that \(Y\) exceeds \(K\) is

\[ P(Y>K) = \Phi\!\left( \frac{\mu-\log K}{\sigma} \right) = 1- \Phi\!\left( \frac{\log K-\mu}{\sigma} \right). \]

Theorem A.12 (Truncated First Moment) The expectation of a truncated lognormal random variable is

\[ E\left[ Y\,\mathbf{1}_{{Y>K}} \right] = e^{\mu+\frac12\sigma^2} \, \Phi\left( \frac{\mu+\sigma^2-\log K}{\sigma} \right). \]

Corollary A.5 (Expected Positive Part) The expectation of the positive part of \(Y-K\) is

\[ E[(Y-K)^+] = e^{\mu+\frac12\sigma^2} \Phi(d_1) - K\Phi(d_2)\, \]

where

\[ d_1 = \frac{\mu+\sigma^2-\log K}{\sigma}\, \]

and

\[ d_2 = \frac{\mu-\log K}{\sigma} = d_1-\sigma . \]
